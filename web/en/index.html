How to check

          What happens to your documents? Nothing.

          • There is no upload. Images are processed by your own browser. We have no server that receives files — this is just a static page.
          • No cookies, no analytics, no account. No third-party scripts, fonts or pixels. The app keeps nothing between visits.
          • Metadata removed. GPS location, phone model and photo date are left out of the PDF; so are the author, company and program of Word documents and opened PDFs. The exception is signing a PDF without changing anything in it: then it stays exactly as it came, and the app tells you first. The PDF carries no name or mark of the app either.
          • Works offline. Once opened, it works even in airplane mode.
          • Blocked by rule, not by promise. The page has a security policy (connect-src 'none') that stops the browser from opening any connection from it.

          You don't have to take our word for it: open the browser tools (F12 → Network tab) while creating a PDF, or read the code. Honest detail: the page host (Cloudflare) sees that someone opened the site, as with any site — but never sees your files.

          Sign with a digital certificate

          Check the address first: the browser bar must show scanner.sankler.com.br. A fake copy of this site could steal the certificate and the password.

          Choose the A1 certificate (a .pfx or .p12 file) and enter its password. The file and the password stay only here on the device and are not kept: next time, the app asks again.

          No file chosen

          Only the A1 certificate, which is a file, works. Tokens, smart cards (A3) and cloud certificates don't work here: use the certificate authority's program.

          Is the certificate installed on your computer, and you don't have the file?

          Browsers don't let any website sign with the certificates installed on your computer. You can export a copy to a file, just once. On Windows:

          1. Press Windows + R, type certmgr.msc and press Enter.
          2. Open Personal and then Certificates. Right-click your certificate, then All Tasks and Export.
          3. Check "Yes, export the private key". In the .pfx format, also check "Include all certificates in the certification path if possible".
          4. Create a strong password, save the file and choose it here, with that password.

          On a Mac, use the Keychain Access app: right-click the certificate and choose Export, in the .p12 format.

          If the option to export the private key is greyed out, the certificate was installed as non-exportable, and only the certificate authority can issue a copy as a file.

          With the file and the password, anyone can sign in your name: keep it somewhere safe or delete it after signing.
